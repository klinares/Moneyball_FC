Research Question

Primary Research Question: How do individual performance metrics and club-level contexts jointly predict player market value trajectories in elite European football?

Specific Hypotheses:

  1. H1 (Performance Effects): To see whether player performance metrics (goals, assists) will have stronger positive associations with market value for attacking positions compared to defensive positions, controlling for other factors.

  2. H2 (Age Effects): Market value will exhibit a curvilinear relationship with age, peaking in players’ mid-20s, reflecting the balance between experience and physical decline.

  3. H3 (Club Context): The proportion of foreign players in a club will positively moderate the relationship between individual performance and market value, as clubs with international rosters may assign higher premiums to proven talent.

Literature Review

1. Valuation in Sports Economics

Herm, S., Callsen-Bracker, H. M., & Kreis, H. (2014). When the crowd evaluates soccer players’ market values: Accuracy and evaluation attributes of an online community. Sport Management Review, 17(4), 484-492.

  • This study here validates the accuracy of Transfermarkt crowd-sourced valuations, finding strong correlations with actual transfer fees (r > 0.90)
  • Establishes the legitimacy of using Transfermarkt data as a proxy for true market value
  • Identifies key attributes that crowds consider such as performance statistics, age, contract length, and club prestige

Key Relevance: Justifies our use of Transfermarkt valuations as the dependent variable and suggests which predictors are empirically relevant.

2. Multilevel Modeling in Sports Analytics

Franck, E., & Nüesch, S. (2012). Talent and/or popularity: What does it take to be a superstar? Economic Inquiry, 50(1), 202-216.

  • The study demonstrates that player salaries (closely related to market value) are influenced by both individual performance and team-level marketing factors
  • Uses hierarchical models to separate individual talent effects from club brand effects
  • Finds that club-level variance accounts for approximately 15-20% of salary variation

Key Relevance: Provides theoretical justification for our three-level model structure (measurements nested in players nested in clubs) and suggests expected variance decomposition patterns.

Connection to Current Study

NOw coming to our study, which extends this literature as:

  1. Longitudinal Design: Unlike cross-sectional studies, we model value trajectories across 12 seasons (2013-2024), allowing us to capture dynamic changes in player worth
  2. Comprehensive Performance Metrics: We include both offensive (goals, assists) and disciplinary (yellow/red cards) measures across all positions
  3. Globalization Effects: We explicitly test whether club internationalization (foreign player proportion) moderates individual performance effects

Expected Contributions

  1. Methodological: We try to demonstrate proper handling of nested vs. cross-classified structures in sports data (players can transfer between clubs)
  2. Substantive: Then, trying to quantify the relative importance of individual talent vs. club context in determining player market value
  3. Practical: Providing evidence-based insights for club transfer strategies and player career management
